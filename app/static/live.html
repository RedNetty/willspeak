How it speaks

The voice everyone else hears, and how long he gets to take it back.
Longer gives him more room to catch a wrong sentence before anyone hears it, but every reply lands that much later and conversation drags. start here: 0 s once you trust it, 1–2 s while you are still testing.
Slower is easier for a stranger to follow in a noisy room; faster sounds more like ordinary conversation. start here: 0.95

Stop phrase

Saving makes it a critical recording prompt. Record it across several sessions so the matcher can recognize it reliably.
Two independent detectors watch for this phrase. Either one firing stops the device, so a garbled transcript can still be caught by his own voice, and the other way round.
Lower catches the stop phrase even when it comes out badly garbled, at the cost of occasionally stopping on ordinary speech. The two mistakes do not cost the same: a missed stop leaves a wrong sentence standing, a false stop is only a moment of silence. The saved default is 0.85. Test both missed stops and false stops before changing it.
This one needs recordings: it compares the audio against his own takes of the phrase from the Record page. It does nothing until he has recorded some. start here: 0.50
A second way for detector 2 to fire: the stop phrase does not have to score high in absolute terms, only clearly higher than everything else he might have said. Set to 0.00 to switch this off and use the score alone. start here: 0.08
Pick a 3-word phrase with different sounds in it ("something is wrong" style, never something he says for real). Record it on several different days, then use "Test: say it now" below to check both detectors catch it; check it in the room and microphone position he actually uses.
Past this window the stop phrase still stops the device, it just does not announce a retraction. By then the conversation has moved on and a late "scratch that" confuses everyone. start here: 6 s
Listens for 6 s, reports whether the stop phrase was detected (and by which detector). Nothing is spoken aloud.

Pause and resume

Two more phrases he never says otherwise. Sleep pauses translation: the device keeps listening but says nothing (it confirms once). Wake resumes it. Both are recording prompts once saved.
This can sit higher than the stop phrase, because both mistakes here are recoverable: a caregiver can flip the state with the button below. Falling asleep by accident mid-conversation is the one to avoid. start here: 0.70
Caregiver override. He does this by voice.

Listening

When the device decides he has started talking, and when he has finished. These two are the usual cause of "it cut me off" and "it keeps triggering on nothing".
Too short and it cuts him off mid-sentence and sends half an utterance. Too long and every reply sits waiting after he has stopped. Slower or more effortful speech needs a longer pause than you would expect. start here: 1.5 s
This is measured against the room's quiet moments, not an absolute level, so it re-learns as the room changes. Move it up if quiet speech is being missed; move it down if the device keeps waking up on background noise. Other people talking count as speech to this slider, so if it keeps picking them up, the fix that works every day is the microphone closer to him, not this slider (see the note below the next one). start here: 10
A safety cap. In a room where other people are talking the device may not hear a pause and would otherwise keep recording; the cap sends what it has so the reply never waits more than a few seconds. Too short and a long sentence gets sent in two pieces. start here: 10 s

Vocabulary

Adding a word queues it for recording; it does not teach it. The matcher only knows a word once he has recorded at least one take of it on the Record page. Two or three takes, on different days, is where it gets reliable.
